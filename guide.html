Habit Guide

Most habits fail in the first two weeks, and usually for reasons you can design around.

Starting

  • Shrink it until it sounds almost too easy. Two pages, not a chapter.
  • Attach it to something you already do without thinking, like your first coffee.
  • Start one habit at a time. Three at once is three chances to quit.

Keeping

  • Set the time and the place in advance, not just the intention.
  • Make the first move friction free. Shoes by the door, book on the pillow.
  • Check in the moment you finish, while it still feels like something you did.

Recovering

  • Never miss twice. One skipped day is noise, two is the new pattern.
  • On a bad day, do the smallest version. One page still counts as watering.
  • If a habit keeps dying, the habit is too big. Cut it in half and replant.

Small and boring beats big and brief.

A habit that survives six months is worth more than one that burns bright for nine days. The version you can do while tired, travelling, or busy is the version that lasts, so build that one first and add to it later.

This is why LifeGarden shows growth in five slow stages instead of one big number. The gap between a sprout and a tree should feel like time well spent, not a score to chase.
